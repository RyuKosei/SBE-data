# Anchor-Aware Geometric Evaluation of Continuous Style Intensity Control in the Qwen Model Family

## Introduction

Controllable generation is commonly evaluated by assigning a text to a discrete style category. This formulation is insufficient when an application asks for an intermediate degree of detail, formality, empathy, humor, expertise, emotional intensity, politeness, or objectivity. A model can produce recognizable endpoint styles and still fail to respond smoothly or accurately to intermediate control values. Continuous style control therefore requires diagnostics that separate at least three questions: whether the response moves in the intended direction, whether it reaches the requested position, and whether it changes in unrelated representational directions.

Scalar judgments from a large language model provide one possible measurement, but they introduce evaluator-model dependence, calibration choices, and cost. A geometric alternative places generated texts between endpoint embeddings. This construction is useful but anchor-sensitive. In particular, endpoints generated by the evaluated model define a local coordinate system and cannot by themselves establish absolute cross-model control quality. Moreover, a straight line between two endpoint embeddings is an approximation over a specified task interval, not evidence that style is globally linear in either representation space or human perception.

We study these issues within the Qwen family rather than pooling unrelated model families. The evaluation combines self-anchored interior calibration error, normalized off-axis drift, trajectory diagnostics, three sentence encoders, self and planned shared anchors, grouped baselines, content preservation, a fixed scalar/content judge, prompt and temperature perturbations, and hardware-matched efficiency records. The study addresses six research questions: continuous calibration (RQ1), the distinction between axial error and off-axis drift (RQ2), anchor sensitivity (RQ3), the adequacy and limits of endpoint-line interpolation (RQ4), within-family scale/version/architecture patterns (RQ5), and quality-efficiency trade-offs (RQ6).

The contributions are: (1) correction of endpoint inclusion and clipping biases in geometric style-control metrics; (2) an anchor-aware diagnostic framework that explicitly limits the interpretation of self-generated endpoints; (3) multi-encoder and empirical-trajectory tests of the straight-line approximation; (4) a five-checkpoint Qwen3.5/Qwen3.6 analysis with scenario-level inference; and (5) reproducible content, robustness, failure-type, and efficiency analyses. The framework is diagnostic. It does not claim that style is intrinsically linear, that the results generalize to all language-model families, or that a small within-family panel establishes a universal scaling law.

## Related Work

### Controllable text generation

Work on controllable generation has used supervised attributes, control tokens, prompting, representation editing, and decoding-time guidance. Most evaluations emphasize endpoint classification or preference, leaving intermediate control values comparatively under-specified. Our setting instead treats the requested style-B strength as a continuous target and evaluates all internal locations without using the endpoints as zero-error observations.

### Style transfer and intensity control

Style transfer traditionally balances attribute change, content preservation, and fluency. Continuous intensity adds calibration and monotonicity: a model may preserve content and express both endpoint styles while compressing most intermediate requests into a narrow response range. We accordingly report calibration slope and intercept, rank and linear correlation, and nonlinear failure patterns in addition to endpoint success.

### LLM evaluation

LLM judges can provide scalar or pairwise assessments at scale but are sensitive to evaluator identity, prompt wording, order, and self-preference. We retain the historical scalar and pairwise Qwen-judge results as auxiliary baselines and use one fixed evaluator configuration for the new corpus. Automatic content judgments are not treated as a substitute for human validation; their accuracy, precision, recall, F1, kappa, and confusion matrix remain explicitly pending until genuine annotations are returned.

### Representation geometry, calibration, and robustness

Embedding-space directions have been used to analyze semantic and stylistic change. A projection onto an endpoint direction supplies a local coordinate, whereas the orthogonal residual captures changes not explained by that direction. Such measurements depend on endpoint separation and encoder geometry. We therefore normalize distances by endpoint separation, retain raw rather than clipped coordinates, report low-separation sensitivity, and repeat the analysis with BGE-M3, the exact historical text2vec checkpoint, and multilingual-E5-large-instruct.

## Methods

### 2.1 Task definition

For scenario (s), style endpoints (A) and (B), model (m), and requested ratio \(\alpha\), the model generates text (T_{s,m,\alpha,r}) for seed (r). The final grid is \(\{0,1/6,2/6,3/6,4/6,5/6,1\}\). The primary estimands use only the five internal ratios. Endpoint texts define coordinates but do not enter the principal mean errors or mean distances.

### 2.2 Dataset and style axes

The dataset contains 320 Chinese scenarios distributed equally across eight axes: concision–detail, emotional apology, empathy–clinical tone, expertise–explanation, formality–request style, humor–neutrality, objectivity–literary expression, and politeness–refusal. Each scenario specifies a fixed semantic requirement, atomic content checkpoints, two style descriptions, and a target Chinese-character interval. The audit confirmed 320 unique scenario identifiers and no malformed source rows (`audit/data_audit.md`).

### 2.3 Qwen model panel

The panel contains Qwen3.5-4B Dense, Qwen3.5-9B Dense, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B MoE, Qwen3.6-27B Dense, and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MoE. Base, reward, embedding, reranker, and vision-language checkpoints were excluded. All included checkpoints were run in BF16 without weight quantization. Qwen3.5 and Qwen3.6 are analyzed separately where scale is discussed, followed by matched or near-matched descriptive comparisons; they are not pooled into a single scaling line.

### 2.4 Generation protocol

Each checkpoint generated 320 scenarios × 7 ratios × 3 seeds = 6,720 outputs, giving 33,600 outputs in total. Temperature was 0.7, top-p was 0.9, maximum output length was 256 tokens, and thinking was disabled. Unlike the legacy run, the three seed values were transmitted to the inference server. The prescribed three seeds were retained for main generation, leave-one-seed-out trajectory analysis, and the prompt/temperature experiment; no additional seed sweep was imposed on deterministic auxiliary analyses.

### 2.5 Self and shared anchors

Under self anchoring, \(z_A\) and \(z_B\) are embeddings of the evaluated model's own ratio-0 and ratio-1 texts. The resulting coordinate measures relative control within that model's local endpoint interval. It is not an absolute cross-model scale. A prespecified subset of 80 scenarios was prepared for model-independent reference endpoints. The shared-anchor workbook requires independent rewriting and two-author checks for factual consistency, isolated style change, sufficient separation, limited length confounding, and absence of new or contradictory facts. No blank or unapproved reference was used in an analysis.

### 2.6 Self-anchored geometric metrics

Let \(\delta=z_B-z_A\). The raw coordinate is \(c_\alpha=((z_\alpha-z_A)^\top\delta)/\|\delta\|^2\). Interior Calibration Error (ICE) is the mean of \(|c_\alpha-\alpha|\) over the five internal ratios. The normalized off-axis drift is \(\|(z_\alpha-z_A)-c_\alpha\delta\|/(\|\delta\|+\epsilon)\), and normalized interpolation distance is the distance to \(z_A+\alpha\delta\) divided by endpoint separation. Out-of-range status is evaluated before clipping. Clipped coordinates are reported only as an ablation. The implementation checks the projection-decomposition residual against (10^{-6}).

### 2.7 Linearity diagnostics

For every seven-point trajectory, we calculate centered-PCA PC1 explained variance, endpoint-to-PC1 alignment, ordered path length divided by endpoint distance, discrete segment curvature, local projection slopes, reversal counts, and internal distances to the endpoint line. A leave-one-seed-out analysis constructs per-ratio empirical centers from two seeds and tests the third. Endpoint-line, discrete-centroid, and piecewise empirical references are compared using separately reported ratio error, nearest-ratio accuracy, and normalized off-trajectory distance; Euclidean target distance is not conflated with ratio error.

### 2.8 Multi-encoder evaluation

All 33,600 outputs were encoded independently with BGE-M3, the exact historical text2vec-base-chinese checkpoint, and multilingual-E5-large-instruct. Pooling, normalization, maximum length, instruction prefix, checkpoint revision, batch size, and device are recorded in `config/encoder_registry.csv` and embedding manifests. Encoder-specific metrics are never averaged before primary reporting.

### 2.9 Baselines and ablations

Baselines include constant 0.5, endpoint-distance ratio, scenario-grouped five-fold length-only regression, scenario-grouped five-fold isotonic calibration of raw projection, and the fixed scalar judge. The constant baseline has MAE 0.260 on the legacy irregular grid but exactly 0.200 on the final sixth grid. Ablations compare seven versus five points, raw versus clipped projection, two versus three encoders, endpoint-line versus empirical references, all axes versus removal of concision–detail, and full data versus removal of the lowest 10% and 20% endpoint separations. Self/shared-anchor comparisons remain conditional on approved shared texts.

### 2.10 Human evaluation

The blinded package contains 16 scenarios (two per axis), four representative models, and five internal ratios, yielding 320 unique texts. Three assignments per text yield 960 formal ratings, with 24 additional attention checks. Raters score style-B intensity (0–100), content coverage (1–5), naturalness (1–5), key-information addition, contradiction, and inability to judge. Model, ratio, seed, and generation identity are hidden. The protocol stores anonymous rater identifiers and durations and fixes exclusion rules before unblinding. No simulated rating is permitted.

### 2.11 Content preservation

Atomic checkpoints are evaluated for every main output by one fixed Qwen3.6-35B-A3B evaluator at temperature 0 with a fixed JSON schema. Coverage is the proportion of checkpoints judged present. A content-preservation pass requires coverage ≥90%, no factual contradiction, no task-changing addition, and no unjudgeable flag. Deterministic diagnostics additionally cover source semantic similarity, character and server-token length, repetition, distinct-2/3, empty response, truncation, and format anomalies.

### 2.12 Statistical analysis

Seeds are first averaged within scenario–model–ratio cells. Scenario is the independent sampling unit, with style axis retained as a bootstrap stratum. Confidence intervals use 10,000 scenario-level resamples. Paired model and method comparisons use the same scenarios, report mean and median differences and paired effect size, and apply Holm correction. The prespecified mixed-effects specification was attempted; it was singular for both projection error and drift. This failure is reported, and scenario-clustered robust OLS is supplied as a documented fallback (`statistics/mixed_effects_results.txt`).

## Results

### 3.1 Endpoint bias correction

The legacy implementation included two endpoint observations whose projection error, off-axis drift, and interpolation distance are zero by construction. For a complete seven-point trajectory, endpoint inclusion therefore multiplies the five-point ICE by exactly 5/7 and understates the internal-ratio error by 28.57%. The historical corrected clipped ICE values were reproduced within the prespecified tolerance of 0.002 for both legacy encoders. Raw projection is used for all new primary results, while clipping produces only a small change because internal extrapolation was uncommon. [Data: `audit/data_audit.md`; `ABL: ablation_5point_7point_raw_clipped.csv`]

### 3.2 Main Qwen-family results

The model ranking by ICE was identical across all three encoders. The three-encoder descriptive means were 0.1561 for Qwen3.5-9B, 0.1728 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, 0.1826 for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, 0.1892 for Qwen3.6-27B, and 0.2018 for Qwen3.5-4B. Encoder-specific Qwen3.5-9B ICE ranged from 0.1485 to 0.1614, whereas Qwen3.5-4B ranged from 0.1946 to 0.2089. This stable ordering supports a model comparison over the present scenarios but not an architecture-independent scaling law. [Data: `MAIN`; `ABL: ablation_two_vs_three_encoders.csv`]

Monotonicity and calibration error were not interchangeable. Qwen3.5-9B had the strongest average monotonicity (0.7995), but its mean normalized drift (0.6802) was slightly higher than the 0.6748 of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Thus, the larger MoE model could move less in orthogonal directions while still have less accurate axial positioning. [Data: `AXIS`, grouped by model]

### 3.3 Anchor sensitivity

The fixed scalar judge estimated mean endpoint style-B separations of 49.46 points for Qwen3.5-4B, 66.75 for Qwen3.5-9B, 71.51 for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, 77.26 for Qwen3.6-27B, and 72.10 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. The especially compressed Qwen3.5-4B endpoint range shows why self anchoring cannot establish absolute control: self projection rescales this narrower model-specific interval to zero and one. [Data: `metrics/self_anchor_pairs.parquet`]

The cross-model shared-anchor comparison is not yet reported: [RESULT: self_shared_anchor_rank_correlation; source=statistics/self_shared_anchor_rankings.csv; status=external_pending]. This placeholder must not be replaced until all 80 reference pairs pass both author checks.

### 3.4 Linearity and nonlinear trajectory

The seven-point trajectories were not generally close to one-dimensional straight paths. Averaged over encoders, model-level PC1 explained variance ranged from 0.349 to 0.408, endpoint-to-PC1 absolute cosine from 0.647 to 0.755, ordered path-length ratio from 4.64 to 5.20, and mean discrete curvature from 116.4° to 118.3°. These diagnostics reject a strong interpretation of globally linear style geometry. They do not make the endpoint projection useless: projection remains a local calibration diagnostic whose failure modes must be reported alongside curvature and drift. [Data: `TRAJ`]

The corrected leave-one-seed-out table reports like-for-like along-trajectory ratio errors for endpoint-line, empirical-centroid, and piecewise references, together with their own nearest-ratio accuracy and off-trajectory drift. [Data: `ABL: ablation_endpoint_line_centroid_piecewise.csv`]

### 3.5 Encoder robustness

Adding multilingual-E5 did not change any model's ICE rank relative to the two-encoder analysis. Absolute ICE and drift values nevertheless differed across encoders, and endpoint-separation dependence remained visible. Endpoint separation correlated negatively with ICE (Spearman −0.317 to −0.413) and more strongly with normalized drift (−0.662 to −0.811), with all corresponding scenario-bootstrap intervals excluding zero. [Data: `tables/three_encoder_ranking_consistency.csv`; `statistics/hypothesis_tests.csv`]

### 3.6 Human validation and content preservation

The fixed evaluator estimated content-preservation pass rates of 98.15% for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, 98.05% for Qwen3.5-4B, 97.72% for Qwen3.5-9B, 96.79% for Qwen3.6-27B, and 98.17% for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. These high automated rates should be interpreted cautiously because evaluator bias has not yet been quantified against the planned human labels. [Data: `CONTENT`]

The scalar judge did not reproduce the geometric ranking exactly. Its lowest overall ratio MAE was 0.2224 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, followed by 0.2274 for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, 0.2286 for Qwen3.6-27B, 0.2330 for Qwen3.5-9B, and 0.2951 for Qwen3.5-4B. This disagreement is evidence that evaluator choice matters rather than evidence that either metric is a human gold standard. [Data: `SCALAR`]

Human agreement and human–geometry validity remain [RESULT: human_agreement_and_geometry; source=statistics/human_rater_reliability.csv and statistics/human_geometry_agreement.csv; status=external_pending].

### 3.7 Prompt and temperature robustness

Across the four representative models, the semantically equivalent P2 template increased fixed-judge style-ratio MAE by 0.0167 (95% CI 0.0065–0.0274), increased content-pass rate by 0.0106 (0.0031–0.0202), and shortened responses by 11.58 characters (−17.57 to −4.61). Lowering temperature from 0.7 to 0.2 had no clear effect on style-ratio MAE (0.0010, −0.0034 to 0.0056) but increased judged naturalness by 0.0410 (0.0171–0.0650). The style-error interaction was 0.0081 (−0.0039 to 0.0199), providing no clear evidence of interaction at the aggregate level. These results show that a meaning-preserving prompt rewrite can shift measured control and response length more than the tested temperature reduction. [Data: `ROBUST`]

All 9,600 required factorial outputs were available. Model-ranking correlations across conditions ranged from 0.40 to 1.00 for scalar style error, whereas naturalness rankings ranged from −0.60 to 1.00 across only four models and should be treated as unstable. Shared-anchor geometric effects remain [RESULT: shared_anchor_prompt_temperature_effects; source=statistics/robustness_shared_anchor_status.csv; status=external_pending] until the reference endpoint review is completed.

### 3.8 Scale, version, and architecture

Increasing total parameter count did not guarantee lower ICE. Qwen3.5-9B outperformed both evaluated 35B-A3B checkpoints, and Qwen3.6-27B had higher ICE than Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. Conversely, drift and ICE produced different orderings. With only five models and strong confounding among version, active parameter count, and Dense/MoE architecture, regression coefficients are descriptive and must not be interpreted causally. [Data: `statistics/scale_version_architecture.csv`; `statistics/mixed_effects_results.txt`]

### 3.9 Failure cases and efficiency

The prespecified flags were frequent: late under-response occurred in 58.7% of scenario–model–encoder cells, curved trajectory in 51.2%, early overshoot in 49.8%, and non-monotonic reversal in 26.6%. Content–style entanglement occurred in 7.6%, length-dominated control in 4.0%, and raw out-of-range extrapolation in 2.8%. The unexpectedly high rate of late under-response cautions against using a favorable average ICE as evidence that individual trajectories are uniformly well behaved. [Data: `FAIL`; `cases/failure_cases.csv`]

On the directly comparable two-L20Z, TP=2, BF16, concurrency-32 setting, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B had P95 latency 3.46 s, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 3.67 s, and Qwen3.6-27B 5.35 s. The 4B and 9B runs used different single-GPU concurrency settings and are plotted descriptively but excluded from direct speed claims. [Data: `EFF`; `statistics/efficiency_pareto.csv`]

## Discussion

Three conclusions are stable in the completed evidence. First, excluding the mechanically perfect endpoints materially changes the magnitude of the reported error: the legacy endpoint-inclusive mean is deterministically optimistic. Second, ratio error and off-axis drift capture related but distinct behavior; their scenario-level Spearman correlation was only 0.338–0.492 across encoders. Third, results depend on the style axis. Concision–detail was the hardest axis, with three-encoder/model pooled ICE 0.2366, drift 0.8960, and monotonicity 0.1375, consistent with its coupling to length and information density. Formality–request was substantially easier (ICE 0.1396 and monotonicity 0.8852). [Data: `statistics/hypothesis_tests.csv`; `AXIS`]

The self-anchor diagnostics clarify the interpretation of apparently good local control. A model may obtain a calibrated coordinate after its own endpoints have been normalized to zero and one even when those endpoints occupy a compressed range under an external evaluator. This is clearest for Qwen3.5-4B. Shared anchors are therefore necessary for absolute cross-model comparisons, but only after their construction is independent of the evaluated models and their content/style quality has been checked.

The trajectory results also define a validity boundary. A straight endpoint direction can summarize local progress, but the low PC1 variance, long ordered paths, and large segment angles show that the full trajectories often bend through the embedding space. Projection and drift should consequently be reported together with empirical-path diagnostics. A low drift/high ratio-error pattern, such as the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B versus Qwen3.5-9B comparison, indicates relatively compact movement in an inaccurate axial schedule; the converse would indicate correct axial placement accompanied by unrelated change.

For model development, these diagnostics imply that endpoint prompting alone is insufficient. Calibration failures can be targeted by ratio-aware training or decoding, while drift and content–style entanglement require constraints on non-target changes. Prompt robustness should be inspected per axis because axes such as detail, humor, empathy, and expertise alter length, information density, or pragmatic strategy rather than a single lexical attribute.

## Limitations and Ethics

The study examines one model family, Chinese tasks, and eight style axes. Sentence-embedding geometry is not human perceptual geometry, and the three encoders share limitations of learned representations. The fixed LLM evaluator may have calibration, self-preference, and family-specific biases. Its high content-pass rates are not accepted as validated until real human annotations are available. Self endpoints introduce model-specific reference ranges, while shared endpoints introduce author-construction choices and are presently awaiting review. The five-model panel is too small and too confounded to support a universal scaling law or a causal Dense/MoE comparison. Dynamic serving configurations also prevent direct latency comparisons outside the explicitly matched TP=2/concurrency-32 group.

The annotation package collects anonymous rater identifiers and timing but no names, contact details, or device identifiers. Ratings must be obtained from consenting adult Chinese-proficient annotators. No artificial or duplicated ratings are used to fill the target sample size.

## Conclusions

Within the evaluated Qwen panel, continuous style control was measurable but neither uniformly calibrated nor globally linear in the examined embedding spaces. Removing endpoint bias increased reported error by the expected deterministic amount, and Qwen3.5-9B achieved the lowest geometric ICE across all three encoders. Larger total parameter counts did not ensure lower error. Off-axis drift, curvature, scalar-judge error, and content preservation supplied complementary evidence and sometimes different model orderings. These findings support an anchor-aware, multi-diagnostic evaluation protocol. Claims about absolute shared-anchor rankings and human perceptual validity are intentionally deferred until the planned external reviews and ratings are complete.
